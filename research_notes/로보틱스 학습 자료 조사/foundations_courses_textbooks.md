# Foundational Robotics Learning Materials: Theory Courses, Textbooks, Lecture Series, and Reference Curricula (as of 2026-11-11)

Scope note: this file covers the *theory/foundations* layer (math, kinematics, dynamics, control, planning, estimation/SLAM, mobile robotics) and how established curricula sequence them. ROS/tooling and hands-on hardware are out of scope except where a foundations course uses them. Target reader: a software developer new to robotics who is designing a beginner-to-intermediate curriculum. Korean-language availability is noted wherever it was found.

Verification caveat: many Coursera/edX/Class Central pages returned HTTP 403/404 to automated fetches on 2026-11-11. Where a page could not be read directly, the status is marked "unverified" and the best available aggregator/secondary evidence is cited.

## Key Question 1: Which textbooks are canonical for robotics fundamentals, and which are free online with companion code/videos?

### Takeaway
*Modern Robotics* (Lynch & Park) is the clear default for a self-taught software developer: the full book, ~98 lecture videos (~7.5 h), a Python library, a free simulator, a Coursera specialization, and an official Korean translation all exist. For estimation, *Probabilistic Robotics* (paid) plus Labbe's free *Kalman and Bayesian Filters in Python* is the standard pairing; for planning, LaValle's *Planning Algorithms* is fully free; Tedrake's *Underactuated Robotics* and *Robotic Manipulation* are free, updated every semester, and come with Drake code. Craig, Spong, Siciliano and Corke are the canonical paid manipulator texts (Craig and Spong are the most-used in university intro courses).

**Kalman and Bayesian Filters in Python (Roger Labbe; free, CC BY 4.0 text / MIT code)**
- ~19.5k GitHub stars, 4.5k forks, 1,440 commits; Jupyter-notebook book covering g-h filter, discrete Bayes, Gaussians, 1-D and multivariate Kalman filters, KF math, designing KFs, nonlinear filtering, UKF, EKF, particle filters, smoothing, adaptive filtering; appendices on H-infinity, ensemble KF, least squares. **Planning Algorithms (LaValle, Cambridge UP, 2006, 842 pp)**
- Entire book free in HTML and as full-book PDFs (US Letter 2-up, A4 2-up, one-page screen layout; files uploaded 11 April 2021) plus per-chapter PDFs. ### Inferences
- A fully free "canonical stack" exists: Modern Robotics (kinematics/dynamics/control/planning intro) → LaValle (planning depth) → Labbe (filters, hands-on) → Tedrake Underactuated/Manipulation (advanced control & manipulation). Only *Probabilistic Robotics* and Barfoot remain paid in the estimation column; Labbe + Stachniss videos (Q3) substitute reasonably at beginner/intermediate level.
- For a Korean learner, Modern Robotics is uniquely well-supported: Korean co-author, Korean print translation (acorn), Korean-subtitled Coursera courses, and SNU exam archives.
- Craig and Spong are what most university intro courses (including Korean ones on KOCW and Michigan AutoRob) assign; a curriculum should name one of them as the "reference text" even if Modern Robotics is the primary.

## Key Question 2: Which MOOCs and OCW courses are most recommended, and what is their status/availability in 2026?

### Takeaway
The Northwestern *Modern Robotics* Coursera specialization is active (session starting 11 Nov 2026, 4.7/5 from 1,259 reviews, 51k enrolled) and is the single most recommended structured course. Penn's Coursera Robotics Specialization pages now return 404 (likely retired; unverified), ETH's edX AMRx shows only an empty shell page (likely archived), and Georgia Tech's *Control of Mobile Robots* closed to new enrollment in 2020. The richest *free* university material is Michigan Robotics' open courses (ROB 101/102/201/311/320/501/502/530), Stanford SEE CS223A (2008 videos), MIT OCW (2.12, 16.410, 6.832, 6.4210), and CMU 16-811 math fundamentals.

## Key Question 4: What math/programming prerequisites do these courses assume, and what resources fill gaps?

### Takeaway
Across Modern Robotics, Michigan, Penn, and Stachniss, the stated floor is: linear algebra (eigenvalues, least squares), single/multivariable calculus, basic ODEs, freshman mechanics, basic probability, and one language (Python or C++). Recommended gap-fillers: 3Blue1Brown *Essence of Linear Algebra*, Strang's MIT 18.06/18.06SC, Michigan ROB 101 (computational linear algebra, only HS algebra needed) and ROB 501, Stachniss's Basics block, and Labbe's notebook book for probability/filtering.

### Cited Findings
- Modern Robotics prerequisites: freshman physics, linear algebra (eigenvalues, positive definiteness), calculus, basic linear ODEs, basic programming; a Linear Algebra Review wiki page is provided. — [Modern Robotics wiki](http://hades.mech.northwestern.edu/index.php/Modern_Robotics). Coursera version: "freshman-level engineering background, including physics, linear algebra, calculus, basic differential equations, and a little programming." — [Coursera specialization](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/modernrobotics)
- Stanford SEE CS223A prerequisite: matrix algebra. — [SEE CS223A](https://see.stanford.edu/Course/CS223A)

### Inferences
- For a software developer, the programming prerequisite is already met; the critical gap-fill is linear algebra at the level of eigen-decomposition, least squares and rotations/homogeneous transforms, plus basic probability (Gaussians, Bayes rule). ROB 101 + 3Blue1Brown + Stachniss Basics covers this in roughly a few weeks; Strang 18.06 is the deeper (full-semester) option.
- Calculus/ODE needs are modest at the beginner-intermediate level (Modern Robotics Ch. 8 dynamics and Ch. 11 control are where ODEs and eigenvalues really bite); a curriculum can defer ODE review until just before the dynamics module.

### Gaps
- No source gave a quantitative time estimate for the math gap-fill (e.g., hours for 3Blue1Brown or ROB 101 self-study); durations here are inferred.

## Key Question 5: How do published roadmaps and degree curricula sequence topics? (2–4 concrete example sequences)

### Takeaway
University curricula converge on the same spine: math foundations (linear algebra, calculus, probability) + programming → kinematics/rigid-body motion → dynamics and control → motion planning → perception/state estimation (filters, SLAM) → mobile robotics / manipulation integration, then specialization. Michigan's open BSE sequence (ROB 101 → 102 → 201/204 → 310/311/314/320/330/340 → 450), CMU's MSR core (Foundations → Sensing & Perception / Thinking About Action / Robot Embodiment / Environment Interaction), Penn's MSE foundations (AI / Robot Design & Analysis / Control / Perception), and the Modern Robotics 6-course MOOC give four concrete, citable sequences.

### Cited Findings

**Sequence A — University of Michigan BSE Robotics (undergraduate, open materials)**
- Math/science: ROB 101 Computational Linear Algebra (or MATH 214/217/417/419) → MATH 115/116 → MATH 215/216 (ROB 201 Calculus for the Modern Engineer can satisfy 115/116/216) → PHYSICS 140/240; intro engineering ENGR 100 + ENGR 101 / ROB 102 / ENGR 151. Core: ROB 204 (4 cr) + three of ROB 310, 311, 314, 320, 330, 340 (12 cr) + EECS 280 (data structures) + probability (IOE 265 / EECS 301 / BIOMEDE 241) + circuits (EECS 215/270) + dynamics (ROB 215 / ME 240) + a "discipline depth" course → TCHNCLCM 350 → ROB 450 Capstone. - Three foundational courses, one from each of at least three of four areas: *AI* (CIS 5190 Applied ML, CIS 5200 ML, CIS 5210 AI, ESE 6500 Learning in Robotics, ESE 6510 Physical Intelligence); *Robot Design & Analysis* (MEAM 5100 Mechatronics, MEAM 5200 Introduction to Robotics, MEAM 6200 Advanced Robotics); *Control* (ESE 5000 Linear Systems, ESE 5050/MEAM 5130 Control Systems Design, MEAM 5170 Control & Optimization in Robotics); *Perception* (CIS 5800 Machine Perception, CIS 5810 CV & Computational Photography, CIS 6800 Advanced Machine Perception); then ≥5 technical electives and ≤2 general electives (≥10 courses implied). — [Penn GRASP curriculum information](https://www.grasp.upenn.edu/academics/masters-degree-program/curriculum-information/)

**Sequence D — Modern Robotics MOOC (self-paced, 6 × 4 weeks)**
- Foundations of Robot Motion (config space, rigid-body motions; Ch. 2–3) → Robot Kinematics (FK, velocity kinematics/statics, IK, closed chains; Ch. 4–7) → Robot Dynamics (open-chain dynamics, trajectory generation; Ch. 8–9) → Motion Planning and Control (Ch. 10–11) → Manipulation and Wheeled Mobile Robots (Ch. 12–13) → Capstone Mobile Manipulation. "Recommended to be taken in order." ~120–153 h. — [Coursera specialization](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/modernrobotics); [Modern Robotics wiki](http://hades.mech.northwestern.edu/index.php/Modern_Robotics)

**Sequence E — Stachniss Online Training (video, estimation-centric)**
- Basics block (linear algebra refs, probability, 3D rotations, homogeneous coordinates, least squares) → Mobile Robotics block (Bayes filter → occupancy grids → locomotion/motion models → observation models → KF/EKF → EKF localization → particle filter/MCL → control → A* → MDPs → ICP → RANSAC → SLAM intro → graph SLAM → landmarks → robust LS) → optional photogrammetric computer vision. — [IPB online training](https://www.ipb.uni-bonn.de/online-training-robotics/)

### Inferences
- A beginner→intermediate curriculum for a developer can mirror Michigan's open spine with free materials: (1) ROB 101 + 3Blue1Brown + Stachniss Basics [math]; (2) Modern Robotics C1–C2 / Lynch videos + Sodemann DH videos [kinematics]; (3) Modern Robotics C3 + Brian Douglas + Brunton Bootcamp [dynamics & control]; (4) Modern Robotics C4 + LaValle Part II + AutoRob projects [planning]; (5) Labbe notebooks + Stachniss Robotics block + ROB 530 homework [estimation/SLAM]; (6) Modern Robotics C5–C6 + Tedrake 6.4210 [mobile robots & manipulation]. This is an inference from the sequences above, not a published curriculum.
- Graduate programs (CMU, Penn) treat "intro robotics/kinematics-dynamics-control" as a single first course and then branch into perception, planning/learning, and embodiment; a self-designed curriculum can adopt the same four-bucket taxonomy for its intermediate tier.
